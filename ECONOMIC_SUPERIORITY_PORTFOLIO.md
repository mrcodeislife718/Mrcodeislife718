# Portfolio Economic Superiority Map

This document converts the portfolio from a collection of technically ambitious systems into a governed economic portfolio. The rule is simple: existing engineering work is preserved; new work is justified only when it increases revenue, margin, retention, distribution, strategic leverage, or enterprise value.

## Portfolio operating rule

Every serious project must answer:

1. Who pays?
2. What measurable outcome are they buying?
3. What existing budget or incumbent does the project displace?
4. What is the highest-value monetization model that fits the product?
5. What creates recurring revenue, expansion revenue, or transaction revenue?
6. What lowers delivery cost or support cost as usage grows?
7. What makes acquisition cheaper or distribution compound?
8. What makes the product hard to replace economically, not merely technically?
9. What is the shortest path from the current implementation to first revenue, $10K MRR, $100K MRR, and $1M+ ARR?

Technical superiority remains necessary. Economic superiority is now a parallel completion requirement.

## Economic portfolio classes

- **R0 — Portfolio/Training:** profile, learning, demonstrations, or support assets that are not standalone businesses.
- **R1 — Cash Engine:** products with the shortest path to direct customer revenue or transaction revenue.
- **R2 — Scalable SaaS/Platform:** products that can become recurring-revenue software or marketplaces.
- **R3 — Enterprise/Infrastructure:** products whose strongest economics are higher-ticket enterprise, usage, API, licensing, managed deployment, or platform contracts.
- **R4 — Strategic IP / Asymmetric Upside:** systems with potentially very large strategic value but longer sales, integration, evidence, regulatory, or market-development cycles.

The same project can move between classes as the market develops. The classification is an economic operating decision, not a statement about technical quality.

## Revenue sequencing

### Wave A — First-dollar / near-term cash

Hired-Ai, JobFlow, IPX, GigFlow, Jiffy-Laundry-App, Nearby, No-Cap, and the most commercially ready lane of Branded should be evaluated first for customer acquisition and paid transactions because their value can be explained directly to a buyer without requiring a long infrastructure procurement cycle.

### Wave B — Recurring software and marketplace revenue

Cortex, Sessions, Codeable, Dev-Zero, Cognified, WorkForce, Muze, Watchable, Knowledge-Base-Ai, skill-x and Boop should be driven toward repeatable subscription, marketplace, transaction, API, or team/enterprise revenue.

### Wave C — High-ticket enterprise infrastructure

Axion, Measure, Syncio, Byte, J.A.R.V.I.S and the integrated developer ecosystem should be packaged for enterprise, API, private deployment, licensing and infrastructure contracts. These should not be forced into low-ARPU consumer pricing.

### Wave D — Strategic/asymmetric technology

Prism, VaultRam, Focus, Maestro, OverDrive, Epiphany, Einstein, ECA-1, G.A.I.A, Bio-Gene, cradleos-v0 and other protected research/IP systems should preserve large upside while extracting commercially bounded capabilities where the buyer and value are clear.

## Economic superiority scorecard

Every product receives a decision score across:

- customer value created;
- willingness to pay;
- revenue recurrence;
- gross-margin potential;
- retention strength;
- expansion potential;
- distribution leverage;
- strategic/IP defensibility;
- implementation readiness;
- sales-cycle length;
- regulatory/compliance burden;
- support burden;
- dependency risk;
- time to first dollar.

Priority is not determined by market size alone. A product with a giant theoretical market but a 24-month path to revenue should not starve a product that can reach customers this month.

## $10K / $100K / $1M economic gates

For each R1-R4 commercial project, maintain three concrete operating targets:

- **$10K MRR / equivalent run rate:** prove a real buyer, repeatable value delivery and a working collection path.
- **$100K MRR / equivalent run rate:** prove repeatable acquisition, retention, support economics and reliable operations.
- **$1M+ ARR and beyond:** prove channel scalability, enterprise expansion, operational leverage and durable competitive positioning.

Infrastructure/IP projects may substitute annual contract value, licensing revenue, usage revenue or signed strategic contracts for MRR where that better matches the business model.

## Anti-patterns

Do not:

- rebuild technically complete work merely to appear busy;
- add features without a buyer/value rationale;
- default every product to a $19 subscription;
- force every repository to become a standalone company;
- treat Stripe or another vendor as the product UI when it should only be a financial rail;
- use tests as a substitute for selling when the product is already technically qualified;
- claim technical or economic superiority without repository/customer evidence;
- spend weeks building private-cloud infrastructure while revenue blockers remain unresolved.

## Portfolio management rule

Every work item must map to at least one of these economic outcomes:

**REVENUE → CONVERSION → RETENTION → EXPANSION → MARGIN → DISTRIBUTION → DEFENSIBILITY → RISK REDUCTION REQUIRED FOR A SALE.**

If a task does not move one of those outcomes and does not protect critical existing functionality, it is deprioritized.
